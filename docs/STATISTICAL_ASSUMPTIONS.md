# Statistical assumptions

- Analyses treat observations as independent. No person identifier, sample design or collection date is available to verify that assumption. Retaining one exact repeat preserves the original denominator, and collapsing it provides a sensitivity check.
- Wilcoxon rank-sum uses pooled ranks and a two-sided test of equal distributions under exchangeability. Under an equal-shape location-shift model, the result can inform a location comparison. Here, differing spreads and tails make the separately estimated median differences more directly interpretable.
- Kruskal–Wallis compares pooled group ranks with tie correction. Interpreting it as a location comparison requires comparable distribution shapes. Six Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise Wilcoxon results reproduce the academic comparisons and remain exploratory because the omnibus rank test did not reject.
- Welch t-tests compare means while allowing unequal variances. Ordinary ANOVA adds a common variance assumption. Welch ANOVA relaxes it. Independence, finite variances and sufficiently well-behaved group means matter for large-sample approximations. A pooled normality diagnostic alone cannot establish the validity of either mean test.
- Spearman rho describes monotonic association using average ranks for ties. The approximate two-sided p-values use a Student t transformation, with a zero-rank-correlation null. Repeated ages and counts make exact untied inference inappropriate.
- The bootstrap treats rows as exchangeable within the relevant sampling unit. Two-group intervals independently resample each group at its observed size. Correlation intervals resample paired rows. With 2,000 draws, percentile intervals reflect sample and Monte Carlo variability. They are marginal 95% intervals and carry no family-wide coverage guarantee.
- Shapiro–Wilk and the Q–Q plot assess pooled charges. IQR fences are descriptive markers. Upper-tail capping and exclusion answer sensitivity questions about modified outcomes or subsets and do not serve as revised primary estimates.
