# Limitations and responsible use

The educational file lacks a known sampling frame, timeframe, claim period, benefit definitions, geography below broad regions, diagnoses, income, policy details and service utilization. Units follow the supplied educational description. Recorded charges have limited relevance to current U.S. premiums, spending or insurer payments.

All comparisons are observational and unadjusted. Age, smoking status and other characteristics can differ across groups. Potential confounding, unidentified repeats, correlated records and unknown selection can affect estimates and uncertainty. Rank tests have different targets from mean tests, and absence of a small p-value is weak evidence for equivalence without an explicit margin and equivalence design.

Binary recorded sex labels simplify demographic categories and may misrepresent identities. Regional and BMI categories can obscure within-group variation. Small combined segments can be unstable. These findings support practice in analytics and communication. Validated representative data, governance and fairness evaluation would be necessary before applying similar methods to operational decisions. This project provides no pricing or underwriting guidance.

The academic report is retained as historical context. See [Data and provenance](../data/README.md) for dataset attribution.
